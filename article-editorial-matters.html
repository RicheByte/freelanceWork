Why editorial matters

November 18, 2025

I learned more about web design from a coffee table book of 1970s magazine spreads than I ever learned from a UX course.

Not user flows or hierarchy or accessibility. I mean the actual thinking. The rhythm. How someone can make you slow down just by making you pause between one thing and the next.

Web courses teach rules. Where buttons go. How to optimize clicks. Guide the eye. All tactical. All useful. Just... they miss something editorial design gets.

Breath matters.

Magazine spreads have silence. White space isn't wasted. It's the thing that makes words next to it hit harder. Give room. Not font size. Space.

The web moved too fast for that. Every pixel fighting for attention. White space became negative. Something to fill.

People are tired though. Reading on screens all day. What if what they actually want is the opposite? Breathing room. Moments where nobody's selling anything.

That's editorial. That's what magazines understood.

Some websites feel alive. Some try too hard. The alive ones have rhythm. They trust empty matters. Let you put the idea down. Don't rush you.

Optimized layout feels like yelling at you. Intentional space feels like someone actually talking.

I started looking at old magazines differently. How they structured things. Made you feel something beyond just extracting data.

The best web design I'd seen pulled from that tradition. Not tactics. Philosophy. How you present changes meaning.

Spacing, pacing. These aren't decorative. They ARE the medium. Change space, change meaning.

Most underrated thing in design. Minimal gets talked about endlessly. But true minimalism isn't removing. It's knowing every element, including empty, has weight. Guide reader not just where to click but how they feel.

The magazine taught me that. Still learning.
