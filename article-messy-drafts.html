Messy first drafts

December 03, 2025

What if we stopped hiding the process.

I spent years perfecting work before anyone saw it. Endless drafts. Rewriting. Cutting. Polishing until every trace of the actual thinking disappeared. And when people saw the final thing, they'd say "wow, how'd you come up with that?"

As if it just appeared. Fully formed. Like magic.

The real answer was boring. I sat with the problem. Got confused. Tried something stupid. Realized it wasn't stupid, it was just unfinished. Kept going. Found one sentence that worked, built from there.

Nobody wanted to hear that though. They wanted the myth. So I gave them polish.

But I started noticing something. The work I was interested in wasn't the finished stuff. It was the behind-the-scenes. The sketches. The voicememos where someone explains what they were trying to do. The tweets where an author argues with themself about how the book should end. The half-baked thoughts that still have energy, still have questions.

That's the interesting part. That's where the actual thinking is.

So what if we flipped it. What if the messy stuff wasn't something to hide. What if it was the feature.

I started publishing rougher work. Unfinished thoughts. Pieces where I changed direction mid-way. Sketches that didn't turn into anything. And something weird happened. More people engaged with it. More people responded. Not in a "wow this is so polished" way. In a "I feel less alone in my own confusion" way.

The messy bits are where the humanity lives. That's where you see someone actually working through something. Not performing mastery. Actually thinking.

I think we're hungry for that. For actual process. For the real conversation instead of the highlight reel. For sketches instead of finished paintings, because sketches show you how the person actually sees.

The first draft is messier. But it has something the polished version lost. The thinking is still visible. The questions are still there. You can follow the logic instead of just accepting the conclusion.

Maybe that's what people actually want.
